# Candidates — One Star, Five Stars for the Food

Judge criteria (in order): (1) one-star alien review of Earth that slips in a food
compliment; (2) sounds like a real alien visitor — distinct voice, outsider
bafflement, a grievance worth one star; (3) lands the joke — complaint and food
compliment both hit, the turn is funny rather than merely stated; (4) one sentence,
inside 200 chars, every word earning its place; (5) fresh angle, not generic
complaints. Craft rule: concrete detail + a twist in the last clause, no template
jokes, no reused numbers, food compliment real and specific.

## C1 — dumplings / glowing rectangles (187 chars)

One star: the locals worship a glowing rectangle, queue for hours to stare at
smaller rectangles, yet their dumplings — pork, steam, chili oil — made me miss
my own planet's cooking less.

Score: (1) yes; (2) strong — rectangle worship is outsider bafflement; (3) weak —
the turn "miss cooking less" is muddled, the joke doesn't land; (4) tight;
(5) screens angle is fresh. Verdict: cut, twist fails.

## C2 — taco / gridlock (181 chars)

One star: I spent three Earth hours trapped in a metal box honking at other metal
boxes, but the roadside taco — charred pork, lime, raw onion — almost made the
gridlock forgivable.

Score: (1) yes; (2) medium — "three Earth hours" is the only alien marker, the
grievance is human-familiar; (3) good — "almost made the gridlock forgivable" is a
real turn; (4) tight; (5) traffic is a common complaint. Verdict: solid, not best.

## C3 — corn / leaking sky (188 chars)

One star: the sky leaks without warning and the locals discuss it endlessly as if
negotiating with it, yet their buttered corn on the cob — salt, smoke, lime —
nearly earned a second star.

Score: (1) yes; (2) strong — "the sky leaks" and negotiating with weather are
genuinely alien readings; (3) good — "nearly earned a second star" ties the twist
to the one-star premise; (4) tight; (5) weather talk is a fresh angle.
Verdict: runner-up.

## C4 — ramen / crash-landing (171 chars) — PICK

One star: this planet's gravity drags my third leg, its nights never truly darken,
but the midnight bowl of ramen — broth, egg, spring onion — was worth the
crash-landing.

Score: (1) yes; (2) strongest — third leg, light pollution, and the crash-landing
frame make the voice unmistakably alien; (3) strongest — "worth the crash-landing"
reverses the whole trip in the last clause; (4) 171 chars, every word earns its
place; (5) fresh — gravity + light pollution + ramen, no generic list.
Verdict: pick.

## C5 — croissant / coffee queue (185 chars)

(3) good — "butter shattering like glass" is vivid, "almost converted me" lands;
